We distinguish RBFOpt with MSRSM and solver-based candidate search from the matched MSRSM variant using genetic candidate search.
The original solver-based observations are retained, and the additional comparison changes only the candidate-search option while preserving the archived initial configurations and evaluation protocol.
Nannicini (2021, Sections 4.2.3 and 5.2) reports difficulties with the nonconvex distance subproblem in the MSRSM--mathematical-solver combination, motivating this configuration comparison without predetermining its outcome on the present cases.
The genetic option does not eliminate auxiliary Ipopt or Bonmin calls in other surrogate-minimization operations.
The uniform baseline evaluates the three shared initial configurations and then samples previously unobserved configurations uniformly without replacement from the complete candidate domain.
The BAM initialization control preserves the archived predetermined callback prefix and continues with uniform sampling without adaptive surrogate modeling.
This control does not separately identify the causal contribution of every BAM component.
The primary timing endpoint is completion of the recorded search stage, excluding subsequent incumbent extraction and export.
Separately measured extraction and export costs for the new runs are reported without assigning zero cost to unmeasured historical operations.
The sensitivity quantity is $T_{\mathrm{search}}+\alpha\sum_i t_i+T_{\mathrm{prep}}$ conditional on a recorded path; it does not predict the path under a different online time limit.
Empirical TTS99 is a restart estimate derived from observed attainment and median completion cost, not a measured 99th percentile or a 99 percent performance guarantee.
